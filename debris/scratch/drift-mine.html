Hazard Design — Drift Mine

Drift Mine

A passive hazard, not an enemy attack - it just drifts, like an asteroid. Shoot it or let anything touch it (a ship, a rock, a UFO) and it detonates in an expanding shockwave; anything else the shockwave reaches - including another mine - goes up a beat later. One well-placed shot into a cluster can clear several rocks at once. One stray collision near a drifting ship can kill it with zero warning beyond the radius it was already standing in.

A chain, live

looping scenario, actual blast-radius scale

A shot clips the left mine. Its shockwave clears two nearby rocks and reaches the second mine, which detonates in turn a beat later and catches a third rock - and very nearly the ship sitting right at the edge of its radius. The dashed rings are always visible, not just during the blast; that is the entire warning a player gets.

Trigger A — Shot

A player's own projectile hitting it detonates it immediately - the deliberate, offensive use: aim for the mine, not the rocks around it.

Trigger B — Collision

Anything physically touching it - a ship, an asteroid drifting into it, a UFO, another mine's shockwave - detonates it identically. No special case by collider type.

Anatomy

idle state, always-visible telegraph

  • Contact spikesEight radiating prongs, slow ambient tumble - the one silhouette in the game that reads as "ordnance" rather than "debris" on shape alone.
  • Warning coreSlow steady pulse in the existing COLORS.ufo red while idle - the same hue every other danger cue in the game already uses.
  • Blast ringA dashed boundary at the exact detonation radius, drawn at all times - the fair-telegraph rule every other hazard in Debris already follows, applied to a threat that can trigger without the player doing anything at all.

Feed the combo multiplier

A mine-triggered multi-kill is exactly the moment ComboMultiplier.ts exists to reward - clearing three rocks in one shockwave should read as a real chain, not three separate, disconnected kills.

Propagation delay, not instant chain

Each hop in the chain fires a beat after the shockwave actually reaches it, not all at once - the delay is what makes a chain look like a chain instead of one simultaneous flash.

Rare, not a minefield

A handful per stage reads as "a notable local hazard." Enough of them and the arena just becomes a no-go zone - the opposite of an interesting decision.

Open question: does it clear Wreck?

Terrain is deliberately non-destructible everywhere else in the game. A mine could be the one deliberate exception - "blow up this cover on purpose" - rather than a loose end to patch later.

Every color above is already in Debris's palette - danger red (COLORS.ufo) for the core and blast, shield blue (COLORS.shield) for the near-miss flash on the ship, metal tones matching the Cardinal's own plating. Nothing new introduced.
