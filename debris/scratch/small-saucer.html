Small Saucer

The classic Asteroids pairing Debris never built: a large, "dumb" saucer that just drifts a straight line and shoots with a sloppy spread (the game's existing UFO, called the Wanderer below for contrast), and a small, accurate one that actively hunts. Live-rendered below, side by side, each running its own real behavior loop rather than a single static pose.

The Wanderer

Straight-line drift, slow ambient tilt/bob. Never reacts to anything. A hazard you route around, not one that reacts to you.

The Small Saucer

Banks into every course correction while closing in, goes dead-still with a glowing nose the instant it locks on, fires once, then disengages. Watch the loop once through.

The attack routine, broken into its four beats

a real sequence, not an arbitrary order

1 / 4

Approach

Steers toward the target, banking into each correction. No weapon active yet - this is the only phase a player can simply out-turn.

2 / 4

Lock-on

Goes completely still, nose fin floods gold (the same accent the Cardinal's own charge-up already uses). This stillness is the entire reaction window.

3 / 4

Fire

One precise shot, nose flashes bright red-white on release. Not a burst - the threat is the accuracy, not the rate of fire.

4 / 4

Cooldown

Nose dims, breaks off to re-establish distance before it can loop back to Approach. No immediate second shot.

Every render above uses only hues already in Debris's palette - hull fill and the saturated red are the existing COLORS.ufo / COLORS.ufoFill the Wanderer already draws with, and the lock-on telegraph reuses COLORS.fractureLauncher gold, the same "something is about to fire" cue the Cardinal boss already trained players on. Nothing here introduces a new color.
